Tmu Ear Violator 4000Streamer

Use your cheap WiFi ear camera without the vendor app. One small, readable program (source-available, free for non-commercial use) that shows the live picture on your phone or computer and never talks to the internet.

⬇ Download for Android 💻 Run on a computer ⌥ Source code

The hardware

One of the many WiFi ear cameras that broadcast an HNDEC-xxxxxx network and normally need the "SL ylz" app.

What it does

🔒

No vendor app

No trackers, ads, analytics or accounts. The Android app asks for network access only.

🔎

Finds your scope

Speaks both known protocols, NE3 and NE7, and detects which one your scope uses.

🧭

Auto-level

Uses the scope's built-in tilt sensor to keep the picture the right way up.

📸

Snapshots and video

Full camera resolution, fullscreen mode, and Square and Circle views. Save stills and recordings.

🩺

Built-in diagnostics

A Log panel with one-click network tests, Reconnect and Restart buttons.

🪶

Tiny

The desktop version is one Python file with no dependencies to install.

Get started

Android (10 or newer)

  1. Download the latest .apk from the Releases page and open it. Allow installing from your browser or file manager when asked.
  2. Turn the scope on: hold its button for 3 seconds.
  3. Join its HNDEC-xxxx WiFi (no password). When Android says there's no internet, choose Stay connected.
  4. Open the app. It finds the scope by itself.

Windows, macOS, Linux

  1. Install Python 3.8 or newer if you don't have it.
  2. Download ne3web.py (right-click → Save link as).
  3. Join the scope's HNDEC-xxxx WiFi.
  4. Run it. A browser tab opens with the picture.
python3 ne3web.py

A plain MJPEG stream for VLC or OBS is at http://localhost:8080/stream.mjpg.

Privacy

StoresOnly the snapshots and recordings you choose to save
CodeAbout 1,250 lines of plain Python you can read, in two files

The scope's own WiFi has no password, so anyone nearby can connect to it while it's switched on. That's the hardware, not this app.

How it works

The scope creates its own WiFi network and sends its picture as JPEG images split into small UDP packets. Two protocols are known:

NE3

The scope sits at 192.168.169.1. Each frame arrives with its JPEG header removed, so the app rebuilds it. The tilt reading rides along in the first packet.

NE7

The scope sits at 192.168.1.1 and answers a broadcast "who's there?" message. Frames arrive as whole JPEGs in numbered chunks, with tilt readings on a separate port.

The app reassembles the frames and shows them in a small web page served from your own device.

Be gentle with your ears

This is a hobby project, not a medical device. Don't push the scope in further than is comfortable, and see a doctor or nurse for pain, discharge, hearing changes, or wax you can't shift safely.
